FURNACE AND AC FILTER REMINDER

How often should you change your furnace or HVAC filter?

A common rule of thumb is to check the filter every month and replace it about every 90 days. Your home may need it more often or less often, so treat that as a starting point and follow the instructions for your equipment and filter.

The bigger problem for most people is not knowing the number. It is remembering to do it.

What changes how often you need a new filter

  • Pets and shedding. More hair and dander usually means checking and changing more often.
  • Dust, renovation and allergies. Dirt-heavy periods clog a filter faster.
  • Filter type. Thin filters often need changing sooner than thick ones. The packaging usually gives a range.
  • How much the system runs. Heavy heating or cooling seasons use filters up faster.
  • Your equipment manual. If it gives an interval, follow it.

A safe way to check

Hold the filter up to the light. If you cannot see light through it, or it looks gray and clogged, it is time. Note the size printed on the frame and the direction of the airflow arrow so the next one goes in correctly. Keep the size written down so you can buy the right one.

If the filter is stuck, the size is unclear, airflow stays weak after replacing it, or the system makes new noises, ices up or leaks, call an HVAC technician. Do not open furnace panels or work on gas or electrical parts yourself.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I change my furnace filter?

A common starting point is to check monthly and replace about every 90 days. Pets, dust, filter type and how much your system runs can shorten that. Follow the guidance for your equipment and filter.

Is the HVAC filter the same as the furnace filter?

In many homes, yes. The same filter sits in the return grille, furnace or air handler and serves both heating and cooling. Check your equipment to confirm where yours lives.

What happens if I never change it?

A clogged filter makes the system work harder and can reduce airflow and air quality. If the system has trouble, ask an HVAC technician.
