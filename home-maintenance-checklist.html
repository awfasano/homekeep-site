HOME MAINTENANCE CHECKLIST

A home maintenance checklist you can actually keep up with.

Most home repairs that cost real money start as small jobs that were skipped. The easiest way to stay ahead is to sort chores by how often they come back, then put each one on a schedule instead of relying on memory.

The list below is a general starting point. Adjust it for your climate, your home, and the instructions that came with your equipment. Skip what does not apply to you.

The checklist, by how often

Every month or two

  • Test smoke and carbon monoxide alarms using the maker's procedure.
  • Check your HVAC filter and replace it when it is dirty or due (many filters are changed every 1 to 3 months; follow the filter and equipment instructions).
  • Look at under-sink areas and around the water heater for drips or damp spots.
  • Run a quick look over the fire extinguisher gauge if you keep one.

Every six months

  • Vacuum or wipe air vents and registers.
  • Flush out the AC condensate drain line if your system has one, following the equipment manual.
  • Review smoke and CO alarm batteries and replacement dates.
  • Look over caulk and seals around tubs, sinks and windows.

Once a year

  • Book a heating-system tune-up before cold weather and an AC tune-up before hot weather.
  • Check gutters and downspouts from the ground, and hire help for anything at height.
  • Locate and test your main water shut-off valve so you know where it is before you need it.
  • Check range hood and dryer vent exits for lint and blockages.
  • Update your home inventory photos and insurance records.

Every few years

  • Plan for alarm replacement when the date printed on the device says so.
  • Ask a professional to look at ducts, the roof and the chimney if you have them.
Gas, electrical, roof and chimney work is for qualified professionals. Use this list to notice problems and book help, not to attempt those repairs yourself.

Why a list alone does not work

A paper checklist tells you what exists, but not what is due this week. Jobs that repeat on different cycles, like a 90-day filter and a yearly tune-up, are easy to lose track of. A reminder that comes back after you finish a task solves that.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I do home maintenance?

It depends on the task. Safety checks and filters come around every month or few months, many systems need a yearly check, and some items only need attention every few years. Sorting tasks by frequency makes it manageable.

What is the most important home maintenance to do?

Safety first: working smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, knowing where your water shut-off is, and keeping heating and cooling equipment serviced and filters changed. Anything involving gas, electrical or roofing should go to a professional.
